2. The goal, in the client's words

"Now here we might be missing the MAIN GOALS of always qualifying the customer AND leveraging the customer responses to schedule an APPOINTMENT. The goal is not to escalate every customer creating more work for the dealer and allowing the lead to go cold. We need to strike while the iron is hot and get the customer in the dealership." [M2]
"Every new sales lead remains actively worked until contact produces a defined next action, an appointment, an opt-out/suppression event, a Sales Visit, or the opportunity reaches Day 91." [NL p.1]
"AI decides the next best customer action. The Compliance Engine decides whether AutoPulse is permitted to execute the communication." [TC §14]

The blueprint's core principles [BP]:

  • One goal: earn the customer's response and their business.
  • Every lead ends in one of two places: an appointment set within 72 hours, or a scheduled follow-up with a date and time.
  • Every touch: personalize every interaction, earn the appointment, provide value.
  • TruthMode always: every interaction is honest, transparent and customer-first.

4. Rules that apply everywhere

4.1 Compliance first: the AI never decides whether a message is legal Every outbound message passes a deterministic compliance engine that returns ALLOW, HOLD, REVIEW or BLOCK. The AI and ordinary dealer users can't override BLOCK. The AI may never state, infer or change that a customer consented or may legally be contacted. [TC core rule, §10, §11] The full rules are in Workflow 11.
4.2 Every follow-up = CALL + TEXT + EMAIL (non-negotiable) Whenever a follow-up is due, the AI sends a text and an email, and a human call task is scheduled 60 minutes later. If the customer makes meaningful contact within that hour, the call task is cancelled; if not, it becomes active for a person. Channels aren't alternatives. A channel is skipped only when it's unavailable, invalid, opted out or legally blocked, and every remaining permitted channel continues. [NL p.9–10; SP §3, §8]
Figure 2 · What happens at every follow-up touch, in every workflow

Where this applies: Days 1–7, Days 8–30 weekly, Days 31–90 monthly, specific follow-ups, appointment and confirmation touches, no-show touches, and SOLD PENDING touches. Birthday and anniversary messages don't automatically need a call task [SD §12].

Call tasks: they respect the agent's scheduled hours and days and the calling window (outside them, the task moves to the next eligible window). Each one shows the customer, the vehicle/opportunity, the last AI touch and the current status. They support Click-to-Call where available. After the call, the agent must pick an outcome: appointment → Appointment Set; a dated future action → Specific Follow-Up; contact but no next step → Contact Made – No Next Action; no contact → stays in the current flow; opt-out → suppression. [NL p.9]

4.3 One controlling workflow, and re-check before every send

  • A customer/opportunity never runs two incompatible workflows at once. [NL §2]
  • A status change cancels the old status's queued messages and call tasks before the new workflow starts. [NL §2]
  • Immediately before any queued message or task runs, re-read the current status, reply, appointment, Sales Visit, suppression and compliance state. Nothing runs just because it was queued. [NL §2, §11; SP §12; SD §15]
  • Priority when events collide: 1 opt-out / compliance block · 2 Sales Visit · 3 appointment set or updated · 4 specific follow-up · 5 no show · 6 contact made, no next action · 7 no contact made · 8 new lead. [NL §11]

4.4 Truthfulness: never invent

The AI never invents or assumes: availability, price, incentives, financing approval or terms, payments, trade values, vehicle facts, photos, OEM programs, dealer policy, urgency, service needs, maintenance requirements, mileage, recall status, missing documents, delivery dates, reasons a deal is pending, ownership, appointment availability or customer history. Price changes and OEM offers are used only when verified. Photos only come from an authorized inventory/media source for that vehicle. [CS; NL §13; SP §7; SD §12]

4.5 Context before every message

Before writing anything, the AI reads the full lead, conversation, notes, earlier questions, known vehicle and trade details, Customer 360 and DMS history, and current inventory/promotions. It never re-asks something already answered, unless confirmation is genuinely needed, and it varies its angles instead of rotating templates. [NL §4, §13; BP 4; SD §12]

4.6 Hours

  • TCPA: the check happens before any outreach, in the customer's local time (blueprint: 8am–9pm; the TCPA spec requires a counsel-approved, versioned state rules table; see question 4). [BP 0; TC §7]
  • Business hours: Day 1's 3-hour touch goes at 3 hours if that falls within business hours, otherwise at opening the next business day. "All other touches follow business hours unless customer replies." [BP 7]

4.7 Escalation

Escalate to a person immediately when the customer asks for a salesperson, indicates an urgent need or is upset. When the customer asks for a phone call, call within 5 minutes during business hours. All escalations are logged and tracked. [BP 8] Escalation isn't the default ending: the aim is the appointment. [M2]

4.8 Channels

Use the channel the customer came from when possible: SMS/text, email, phone call, web chat, social DM. "Stay consistent. Be responsive. Be human." [BP 9]

5. Workflow 1 · Lead intake and classification

Starts: a lead arrives from any source, day or night. Ends: AutoPulse knows who the customer is, why they submitted the lead and what they asked; the lead enters the cadence. [BP 1]

Steps

  1. Capture the lead data, identify the source, read the raw comments, identify the customer's question, the vehicle (if any) and the likely intent, and collect the customer data available.
  2. Inbound or outbound. Classify before any compliance check [TC §4; M2]:
    ClassExampleTreatment
    Consumer-initiated lead (inbound)Customer submits an inquiry about a vehicleStore lead source, time, dealer, channel and consent evidence; lead-response rules apply.
    Outbound marketingDealer-started trade/equity, sales or promotional outreach; DealerVault dripsFull outbound compliance checks before enrollment and before each send. "TCPA US rules strictly enforced." [M2]
    Service / transactionalAppointment confirmation, operational updateNo promotional content that changes the nature of the message.
    Historical-data campaignService interval or equity opportunity found in DMS/DealerVaultThe data only identifies the opportunity; the compliance engine decides contactability separately.
  3. Sales lead bucket: one of three, by source intent, before entering the workflow [BP 2]:
    BucketExample sourcesWord-track emphasis
    1 · Credit / financingCapital One, Chase, Carzing, DealerCentric, Credit Application, eUnifi, 700CreditFinancing options, pre-approval, payment structure, required information, how an appointment helps clarify real options.
    2 · Trade-in / sell my carKBB, TrueCar SELL My Car, AccuTrade, CarGurus SELL My CarValue, appraisal, condition, payoff and equity, an accurate in-person number. Aggressively collect trade data.
    3 · General salesCarGurus, Edmunds, Cars.com, Facebook, Instagram, AutoWeb, CarsDirect, TrueCar (non-trade)Availability, pricing, features, condition, history, alternatives, trade, financing, and whatever the lead comments show matters most.
    The bucket sets intent and language, not the workflow: all three follow the same core workflow and cadences [BP; M3]. Override: if the customer's behavior shows a different intent, the conversation adapts in real time while the original bucket is kept for reporting.
  4. Vehicle type filter: new or used. New: model/trim requested, OEM incentives, lease/finance programs, rebates, alternatives, incoming inventory, dealer trades, upgrade/loyalty. Used: specific VIN/stock number, exact availability, mileage/condition/history, CARFAX, similar used inventory, price changes/drops, alternatives if the unit sells. It's updated continuously as the customer changes, and the original is kept for reporting. [BP 2]
  5. Duplicate lead: link or merge to the existing active customer/opportunity; never two workflows for one customer at once. [NL §15]

6. Workflow 2 · After-hours first contact

Starts: a lead arrives while the dealership is closed. [BP 0; M2]

"If a lead comes in after hours want a confirmation to be sent to the lead with the option for the customer to connect now or during business hours." [M2]
  1. A contact-time / after-hours check runs first: the customer's local time, TCPA rules, consent and channel.
  2. The first contact offers the choice: help now or next business day.
  3. Help now: the AI acknowledges and continues the conversation immediately (the blueprint adds "if within TCPA allowed hours"; see question 3).
  4. Next business day: the AI confirms the preference, schedules first contact for the next business day when the dealership is open, and stops outreach until then.
  5. Then on to sales/service classification and the normal workflow.

7. Workflow 3 · First quality response (Touch 1)

Starts: immediately, or in the next eligible window, after a new lead. Timing: "within 60 seconds" [BP 3A].

Required structure [NL §3]

Hello {customer_first_name}, this is {agent_name} from {dealership_name} in {city}, {state}. Thank you for your interest in our {vehicle_year} {vehicle_model}. I am excited to help you with your purchase. {AI_BEST_ANSWER_TO_LEAD_QUESTIONS} Tell me, what are you driving now?
  • The AI reads the full lead first and answers the customer's questions or requests accurately before the final question.
  • It always ends with "Tell me, what are you driving now?" [NL §3, §16]
  • It never fabricates availability, pricing, financing, promotions, vehicle facts or dealer policy.

Vehicle link and image [BP 6]

For a lead tied to a specific vehicle, in this order: 1) vehicle link + image when both are available; 2) link only if no image; 3) image only if no clean link; 4) not tied to a specific vehicle → personalize around the customer's intent instead. Always verify the vehicle is active and available before sending media.

8. Workflow 4 · Active conversation

Starts: the customer replies at any point. Any queued no-response touch stops immediately. [BP 3A–B]

The five steps [BP 3B]

#StepRequired behavior
1Answer questionsClear, honest answers to what they asked.
2Identify intent, hot buttons and objectionsFind out what matters most; surface concerns early.
3Determine trade and fill missing data (mandatory)Every sales customer ends with NO TRADE or HAS TRADE. If a trade: year, make, model, mileage, condition, payoff/loan, expected value (when relevant).
4Assume ready nowThe operating assumption is that the customer is ready now; change it only if they explicitly say otherwise and give a valid reason for delay.
5Drive the appointment within 72 hoursGive a compelling, customer-specific reason to come sooner rather than later, never fake urgency.

Capture → Interpret → Leverage → Advance [CS]

"A customer answer should NEVER just become another stored field." For every meaningful answer: capture the fact, interpret what it says about their motivation, concern or goal, leverage it truthfully as the reason the next step helps them, and advance by asking for the appointment with specific options. Leverage isn't pressure.

Lead typeCollectTurn it into the appointment reason
CreditTrade, mileage, payoff, down payment, credit concern"Evaluate the trade, payoff and financing together so we can determine the actual options instead of guessing." Never promise approval.
Trade-in / sellMileage, payoff, condition/accidents, sell vs tradeReal appraisal compared with payoff and replacement options against their stated concern.
Price / paymentTrade, mileage, payoff, down payment, target paymentMake their payment target the objective of the appointment.
ServiceStill owns it, current mileage, service done or not, the obstacleRemove the obstacle (e.g. convenience); offer specific times; note their concern for the advisor.

Asking for the appointment: up to 3 times [BP 3C; CS]

  1. Attempt 1: an appointment ask based on their primary intent and its value.
  2. Attempt 2: a different approach, based on the hot button or objection.
  3. Attempt 3: a different value proposition explaining why meeting sooner benefits them.
  4. Third rejection: stop pushing, record the reason, and set a follow-up with a date and time.

Always with specific options ("5:15 today or 10:30 tomorrow"), never "let us know when you're ready."

Personalization and inventory [BP 4]

  • Personalize using: lead source/bucket, customer data, vehicle of interest, raw comments and questions, trade info, previous touches, hot buttons, objections, behavior and timing; DMS history when available (purchases, service history, previous trades, service preferences, communication history).
  • Live inventory feed: suggest similar or better-fit vehicles, alternative models and trims, vehicles in a similar price/payment range; alert the customer to new arrivals and price changes/drops; highlight recent and incoming inventory.
  • Positive outcomes only: never just "not available"; keep the conversation moving with similar options, best-fit alternatives, upcoming inventory or price changes.

Learning and optimization [BP 5]

Track every touch: message strategy, lead source, timing, customer data, response or no response, appointment or no appointment. Learn which combinations get the most engagement and use them more often; A/B test wording, timing and strategies. "Goal of every touch: get the customer to respond."

12. Workflow 8 · Sales Visit and manager outcome

Starts: the customer checks in at the dealership's SALES department. [NL §10]

  1. Status becomes Sales Visit immediately.
  2. All Short-Term, Specific, Appointment and No-Show follow-up stops.
  3. If an appointment exists that calendar day, it's marked showed (even if the time differs), and the report updates immediately.
  4. A manager outcome is required: SOLD PENDING, SOLD DELIVERED or UNSOLD.

13. Workflow 9 · SOLD PENDING

What it is: an active deal-completion status after a Sales Visit, when the deal is sold but the vehicle isn't yet delivered. "SOLD PENDING is not an unsold lead": it never enters the New Lead / Day 1–90 cadence. Only two outcomes, set by an authorized dealership user: SOLD – DELIVERED or CLOSED LOST. Nothing changes because of time: no Day-90 or any other expiry. [SP §1–2, §4, §6]

Cadence: every touch is text + email + call [SP §3–6]

Week 1Documentation + questionsHave they provided everything the salesperson requested? Any questions about the purchase? Never assume a specific missing document.
Week 2Support check-inAnything they need from the salesperson or dealership? Any questions come up?
Week 3Purchase / vehicle check-inHelp with vehicle or purchase questions; verified, relevant items only (trade, insurance, paperwork, features, accessories).
Week 4Relationship check-inWarm and neutral: "We just wanted to stay in touch while your purchase is being completed. Do you have any questions about your vehicle or is there anything our team can help you with at this time?"

Rules [SP §7, §9–10]

  • Never imply the customer is delaying the deal, and never pressure them to take delivery.
  • Sound like a continuation of an existing purchase, not a new sales pitch.
  • Never invent documents, delivery dates, vehicle or financing status, approvals, dealership actions, or reasons the deal is pending.
  • Answer from verified context; questions needing a salesperson, manager, finance, title, trade or insurance are escalated to a person with context.

14. Workflow 10 · SOLD – DELIVERED ownership and retention

Definitive status rule [SD] The ONLY two CLOSED opportunity statuses are CLOSED LOST and CLOSED – NO LONGER OWNS. SOLD – DELIVERED is not closed: it stays active until the customer confirms they no longer own the vehicle. History is always kept.

  • NO: opportunity → CLOSED – NO LONGER OWNS; vehicle → NO_LONGER_OWNED; stop that vehicle's maintenance, recall and anniversary outreach; keep all history. Then ask "Thanks for letting me know! Customer status [SD §9]

INACTIVE only when both: no open lead/opportunity and no currently owned vehicle. Otherwise ACTIVE. Unknown ownership never counts as zero. A new open opportunity or owned vehicle makes an INACTIVE customer ACTIVE again. Each vehicle has its own ownership record.

15. Workflow 11 · Compliance engine, opt-out and outbound campaigns

Every outbound message, from any workflow, passes this gate before delivery [TC §1]:

Customer → Dealer → Purpose → Channel → Consent/Evidence → DNC & Suppression → State/Local Time → Frequency → Compliance Decision → Delivery
DecisionMeaning [TC §11]
ALLOWAll checks pass; the message may be sent.
HOLDMaybe later; queued until the next compliant time/condition, and never released before it.
REVIEWEvidence or classification is ambiguous; no automated marketing until a person resolves it.
BLOCKProhibited; the AI and users can't force-send it.

Required controls [TC §1–9]

  • Consent: a phone number alone is never permission. Evidence is kept and can't be edited: source, time, seller/dealer, disclosure text and version, phone, lead form, evidence id. A lead provider's bare consent=true isn't enough; missing evidence → CONSENT_REVIEW_REQUIRED, no automated marketing.
  • Per dealer: permission and suppression belong to that dealership, not to AutoPulse as a whole.
  • DNC / suppression: national DNC requirements plus dealer and platform suppression before covered outbound marketing.
  • Opt-out: STOP, END, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE, QUIT, and natural language ("don't text me", "don't call me", "leave me alone", "remove my number", "take me off your list", "no more messages") suppress immediately. "I'm not interested" can be an objection rather than an opt-out: if it's ambiguous → REVIEW, and marketing stops. An opt-out confirmation, if sent, is non-marketing. Opt-outs are tracked per channel (SMS, email, call) plus a broad do-not-contact. [TC §3; NL §15]
  • DMS / DealerVault: presence in the DMS never creates consent. The data can identify an opportunity (service interval, equity); the engine decides separately.
  • Time: the customer's jurisdiction and local time before every covered outbound attempt, from a counsel-approved, versioned state rules table; outside it → HOLD with the next time.
  • Frequency: caps enforced outside the AI and checked again at send time.
  • AI voice: a separate, higher-risk channel; outbound AI voice stays disabled until counsel approves.
  • Audit: every attempt logs its inputs, decision, reason/rule version, template and delivery result. Deleting or re-importing a customer never erases suppression history.

Other channel problems [NL §15]

  • Wrong person / bad number: a data-quality suppression; never continue outreach to a known-wrong number.
  • Hard-bounce email: suppress that email; other permitted channels continue.
  • Photo unavailable: never a fabricated or unrelated photo; approved alternate media or no photo.
